Harajuku, Shibuya & Shinjuku

Today covers Tokyo's most iconic neighbourhoods — a peaceful forest shrine, the world's most famous crossing, and the dazzling neon of Shinjuku at night.

📍 View on Google Maps →
1:30 PM
Kasugataisha Shrine
Deep in ancient forest, lined with thousands of moss-covered stone lanterns. One of Japan's most sacred Shinto shrines. Utterly serene.
📍 View on Google Maps →
3:30 PM
Naramachi District
Try kakinoha-zushi (sushi in persimmon leaves) and shika manju (deer-shaped buns) before taking the Shinkansen to Kyoto.

Fushimi Inari & Gion

⛩️ Kyoto

Kyoto's spiritual heart — rise before the sun for one of Japan's most iconic hikes, then spend the evening in the historic geisha district as lanterns flicker to life.

5:30 AM
Fushimi Inari Taisha
⚠️ Go at sunrise — by 9am it's packed. Thousands of vermilion torii gates wind up the mountain. The full hike takes 2–3 hours. Open 24h.
📍 View on Google Maps →
10:00 AM
Nishiki Market
"Kyoto's Kitchen" — a 400-year-old covered market with pickled vegetables, matcha soft serve, grilled skewers, and every Kyoto specialty.
2:00 PM
Kiyomizudera Temple
Perched on a hillside with dramatic views of Kyoto. The wooden stage extends out over the cliff without a single nail — incredible engineering.
6:00 PM
Gion District — Hanamikoji Street
Walk at dusk for the best chance to spot geiko and maiko in kimono. Respect the strict no-photography rules — just absorb the atmosphere.
Fushimi Inari Taisha · 10,000 torii gates climbing Mount Inari

Arashiyama & Kinkaku-ji

⛩️ Kyoto

Two of Kyoto's most breathtaking sights — a bamboo grove at dawn so quiet you can hear the stalks creak, and a gold-plated pavilion shimmering on a mirror-still pond.

6:30 AM
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove
Go before 8am for a surreal, near-empty experience. The bamboo reaches 30m — light filters through in an otherworldly way. Walk to Tenryu-ji garden after.
9:30 AM
Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)
Japan's most photographed sight. The gold reflection on Kyoko-chi pond is best in morning light. Budget 1 hour — the grounds are beautiful.
1:00 PM
Kaiseki Lunch
Splurge on a kaiseki multi-course lunch — even a mid-range one (¥3,000–5,000) in Kyoto is life-changing. Book ahead.
4:00 PM
Philosopher's Path
A canal-side stone path connecting Ginkaku-ji to Nanzen-ji. Lined with cherry trees, boutique cafes, and small pottery studios. Perfect late-afternoon walk.
Kasugataisha · Lantern-lined paths through ancient sacred forest

6:00 PM
Dotonbori at Night
The Glico Running Man sign, giant mechanical crab, neon chaos. Eat takoyaki, okonomiyaki, and kushikatsu along the canal. Never double-dip the sauce!

What to Eat

Food You Must Try

Japan has the most Michelin stars in the world — but its greatest food is often found at street stalls and tiny counter seats.

🗼 Tokyo

🍜
Must Try
Ramen
Order from the ticket machine, grab a counter seat, and slurp loudly — it's polite. Try tonkotsu (rich pork broth) or Tokyo-style shoyu.
🐟
Must Try
Tsukiji Sushi
The freshest tuna in the world, served at the outer market stalls. A medium toro hand roll at 8am is one of life's great pleasures.
🥚
Tamagoyaki Skewer
Sweet, soft rolled egg on a stick from Nakamise-dori stalls in Asakusa. Simple, fresh, and utterly Japanese.
🍣
Omakase Sushi
A chef's-choice tasting menu at a counter-seat sushi bar. Even a mid-range omakase (¥5,000–10,000) will rearrange your concept of sushi.

🦌 Nara

🍱
Must Try
Kakinoha-zushi
Nara's signature dish — pressed sushi wrapped in preserved persimmon leaves. The tannins in the leaf perfume the fish subtly. Available at Hiraso.
🦌
Must Try
Shika Manju
Deer-shaped steamed buns sold at stalls near the park entrance. Light, fluffy, and filled with sweet bean paste. Perfect travel snack.

⛩️ Kyoto

🍵
Must Try
Matcha Everything
Kyoto is the home of ceremonial matcha. Try soft serve, wagashi sweets, latte, and a proper whisked bowl at Ippodo or Nakamura Tokichi.
🍽️
Must Try
Kaiseki Dinner
Japan's haute cuisine — a seasonal multi-course meal where every dish is a small work of art. Book ahead; even a mid-range kaiseki is life-changing.
🥢
Yudofu
Silken tofu simmered in kombu broth — a Kyoto classic that proves simplicity is its own form of perfection. Best at Tousuiro near Kiyomizudera.
🥬
Kyoto Tsukemono
Pickled vegetables unique to Kyoto — shibazuke, suguki, and senmaizuke. Pick up a tasting pack at Nishiki Market to bring home.

🍜 Osaka

🐙
Must Try
Takoyaki
Osaka invented these octopus balls. Watch them cook on the iron mold, then drown them in bonito, mayo, and okonomi sauce. Eat immediately — scalding hot!
🥞
Must Try
Okonomiyaki
A savory pancake packed with cabbage, pork, and topped with a glossy sweet sauce. Osaka-style (mixed together) is different from Hiroshima-style (layered).
🍢
Must Try
Kushikatsu
Deep-fried skewers of meat, seafood, and vegetables. Sacred rule: NEVER double-dip in the communal sauce. Never. Eat at Daruma near Dotonbori.
🥩
Yakiniku BBQ
Korean-style BBQ at your own grill — huge in Osaka's Tsuruhashi district, Japan's largest Korean town. Order wagyu A5 at least once.

Getting Around

How to Travel

Japan's transport is world-class — punctual to the minute, comfortable, and surprisingly easy to navigate with the right prep.

🚅
JR Pass — Buy Before You Leave
The Japan Rail Pass covers all Shinkansen (bullet trains) between cities and most JR local trains. A 14-day pass costs ~¥50,000 (~$330). Buy it in your home country before departure — it cannot be purchased inside Japan at the same price. Activate it at any major JR station upon arrival.
💳
IC Card — Suica or Pasmo
A rechargeable card for all metro, bus, and local trains within cities. Also accepted at convenience stores, vending machines, and many restaurants. Add it to your iPhone Wallet for tap-and-go. Top up at any station machine. Works in Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, and Nara.
🚇
City Metro
Every city has an excellent metro system. Tokyo alone has 13 lines. Google Maps works perfectly — just enter your destination and it tells you exactly which train, which platform, and where to stand on the platform to be closest to the exit.

✈️ Getting from Tokyo Airport

Narita Airport (NRT)
Narita Express (N'EX) → Tokyo Station: ~55 min. Covered by JR Pass. Runs every 30 min.
Haneda Airport (HND)
Tokyo Monorail or Keikyu Line → central Tokyo: ~30 min. Cheaper and faster than NRT.
Osaka KIX Airport
Haruka Express → Shin-Osaka: ~75 min. Namba by Nankai Rapid: ~40 min (not JR Pass).
Pocket WiFi Tip
Pre-order a pocket WiFi device or SIM card to the airport counter before leaving home. Pick up on arrival — essential for navigation.

Before You Go

Essential Tips

Small things that make a huge difference — save these before you leave.

💴
Carry Cash — Always
Japan is still very cash-heavy. Many restaurants, shrines, and small shops are cash-only. 7-Eleven ATMs accept foreign cards reliably. Withdraw ¥30,000–50,000 at the airport on arrival.
🦌
Bow to the Nara Deer First
Hold out one cracker, bow slowly — many deer will bow back before taking it. Never hold the whole stack visible or you'll be swarmed. Show both empty palms when done.
⛩️
Visit Shrines at Dawn
Fushimi Inari, Sensō-ji, and Meiji Jingu are overwhelmingly crowded by 10am. Going at 5–7am gives you an almost private experience that's completely transformative.
👟
Wear Slip-On Shoes
You'll remove your shoes at temples, traditional restaurants, and ryokan inns — sometimes dozens of times a day. Wear comfortable, easy-to-remove shoes. Your feet will also walk 20,000+ steps daily.
🎟️
Pre-Book Key Attractions
teamLab Borderless sells out weeks ahead. Certain ramen restaurants and sushi counters require reservations months in advance. Book before you leave home — don't assume walk-ins work.
🗑️
Pack Your Trash
There are almost no public bins in Japan. Streets are spotless because everyone carries their rubbish to a convenience store bin or back to the hotel. Bring a small bag for waste — it's the respectful thing to do.
🍢
The Kushikatsu Rule
At kushikatsu restaurants in Osaka, the communal dipping sauce is shared. The sacred, inviolable rule: NEVER double-dip a skewer you've already bitten. Use the provided cabbage to add more sauce if needed.
📱
Google Translate Camera
Point your phone camera at any Japanese text and it translates in real-time. Indispensable for menus, signs, and train displays. Works offline if you download the Japanese pack in advance.
